Certificates open doors. They don't make anyone good. All prices and validity periods are approximate as of September 2026 and move constantly: confirm on the official page and ask for student pricing.

## Five rules that prevent expensive mistakes

1. **Two or three certifications in 24 months at most.** Each should cost under 20% of your total study hours.
2. **Never buy a certification you can't already half-pass.** They validate skills; they're terrible at building them.
3. **Pair every certification with a project** showing the same ability.
4. **Let job adverts decide, not forums.** Twenty adverts in your target market will show which badges are filters there.
5. **Prices, versions and retirement dates move constantly.** Verify before paying.

## What actually filters CVs

- **Morocco and francophone markets:** ISO/IEC 27001 carries real weight in consulting, banking and telecom GRC. Microsoft and Cisco are widely recognised by integrators. OSCP dominates pentest consultancies. French proficiency is itself a hiring criterion.
- **Europe (remote or relocation):** practical certifications (OSCP, CPTS, BTL1, BSCP) and cloud certifications; ISO 27001 and CISM for governance.
- **North America and defence-adjacent:** Security+ is still a formal baseline filter in many job families.
- **Startups and product companies anywhere:** portfolio, GitHub and a good technical conversation outweigh every certificate here.

## Preparing without wasting months

Download the official objectives and turn them into a checklist. Score yourself honestly and study only the gaps. Use practice exams to find weaknesses, not to build confidence. Simulate exam conditions at least twice: timed, no notes, no AI. For practical exams, practise the report too: more candidates fail on documentation than on access.

?? On a practical exam, which preparation does the roadmap warn people neglect?
+ Writing the report, not only the exploitation
- Memorising every single tool's complete command list
- Buying the most expensive training bundle available
- Reading forum posts about the exam content
