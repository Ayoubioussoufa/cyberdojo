The plan only works if the hours are scheduled, not hoped for.

## The default week: about 13 hours

| Day | Time | Focus | What you actually do |
|---|---|---|---|
| Monday | 1.5 h | Theory | Read or watch the month's core material, notes in your own words, 3 Anki cards |
| Tuesday | 1.5 h | Guided labs | Platform rooms for the month's topic, lab notes as you go |
| Wednesday | 1 h | Coding | Scripting or project code, small committed increments |
| Thursday | 1.5 h | Unguided labs | A machine or challenge with no walkthrough: this is where competence forms |
| Friday | 1 h | AI + news + recall | 30 min AI exercise, 15 min news, 15 min Anki |
| Saturday | 4 h | Project + CTF | 2.5 h portfolio project, 1.5 h CTF or hard challenge |
| Sunday | 2.5 h | Write-up, review, career | 1.5 h documentation, 30 min review, 30 min career action |

::: key Two non-negotiables
The unguided session on Thursday, and the Sunday review: what I learned, what's blocked, three priorities for next week. Everything else can move.
:::

## How the mix shifts

| Phase | Theory | Labs | Projects | Coding | Reading/CTF/AI | Career |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 (M1–6) | 25% | 35% | 15% | 15% | 5% | 5% |
| 2 (M7–12) | 20% | 30% | 25% | 10% | 10% | 5% |
| 3 (M13–18) | 15% | 25% | 35% | 10% | 10% | 5% |
| 4 (M19–24) | 10% | 20% | 35% | 5% | 10% | 20% |

## The 5-hour week: minimum viable progress

- **When:** exam-heavy semesters, a demanding internship, family obligations, low-energy months. It's a legitimate mode, not failure.
- **Structure:** two weekday sessions of 1 hour (one labs, one project), plus one weekend block of 3 hours.
- **Cut in this order:** stretch labs → CTFs → theory reading → secondary tools. Never cut the milestone items, the write-up or the weekly review.
- **Expect** the timeline to stretch to 40–48 months, or keep 24 and do only the core months: 1–12, 13–14, 17–18, then your track.
- **Protect one project per two months.** Without a shipped artifact, low-volume months leave no trace on your CV.

## The 20-hour week: accelerated

- **When:** a light semester, a summer, or job-hunting after graduation. Don't sustain it for more than three months.
- **Add:** a weekly team CTF (3 h), a second unguided lab (2 h), open-source or research (2 h). Not more courses.
- **Burnout guard:** one full day off every week, sleep before labs, and a hard stop when quality drops.

## Modes

| Mode | When | What it looks like |
|---|---|---|
| **Exam mode** | 2–3 weeks a semester | 3–5 h/week: Anki, one lab, keep notes current. No new topics. University comes first: the degree is part of the strategy |
| **Internship mode** | A full-time 4–6 month PFE | 5–8 h/week: the internship is the lab. Keep a private journal with no confidential data, and make the project your capstone |
| **Recovery mode** | After illness or a motivation dip | One week of maintenance: re-read notes, redo a familiar lab, publish a short post. Restart the following Monday |

## Three habits that matter more than the schedule

- **Same time, same place.** Decision fatigue kills more study plans than difficulty does.
- **Always stop mid-task.** A half-finished lab makes restarting trivial.
- **Track hours by category.** At month end, compare with the target mix. Most people find they're 70% theory and 10% projects, which explains why labs feel hard.
