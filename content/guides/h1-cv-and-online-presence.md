Being good is necessary and not sufficient. This guide and the next three cover how to turn skill into offers. In the tables below, M6 means Month 6 of your roadmap, and M6–M8 means Months 6 to 8.

## Career actions by month

| Months | Actions |
|---|---|
| M1–M3 | Create GitHub, LinkedIn and a portfolio site. Join two communities. Start write-ups |
| M4–M6 | Publish monthly. Attend one event. Find a CTF team. CV v0 as a working document |
| M7–M9 | CV v1. Apply for summer internships 3–4 months ahead. One informational interview a month |
| M10–M12 | Internship or project sprint. Midpoint portfolio review. Ask a practitioner to review your CV |
| M13–M16 | Final-year internship (PFE) applications. Submit a lightning-talk proposal. Look for a mentor |
| M17–M20 | CV v2 aligned with your track. Portfolio site with six projects. 5–10 tailored applications a week |
| M21–M22 | Capstone and certification. Ask for referrals explicitly. Apply to bridge roles too |
| M23–M24 | Interview sprint, mock interviews, negotiation prep, thank-you notes, retrospective |

## The CV

- **One page** with no professional experience, two at most ever. Plain formatting: no tables, columns or skill-rating bars. Applicant tracking systems mangle them and reviewers distrust them.
- **Order for a student:** header with links → two-line summary naming the target role → Projects → Skills → Education → Certifications → Languages. Projects come before education because they're the evidence.
- **Bullets follow action + tool + result + number.**
  - Weak: "Worked with SIEM tools."
  - Strong: "Built a Wazuh SIEM ingesting Sysmon, auditd and firewall logs; wrote 15 ATT&CK-mapped detections and validated each with Atomic Red Team."
- **Tailor per application.** Mirror the advert's vocabulary where honest, and drop what's irrelevant.
- **Languages matter** in North Africa and Europe. State French, English and Arabic levels, and keep a French CV if applying locally.
- **No lying, no inflation.** "Familiar with" is fine. Claiming production experience you don't have ends interviews badly.

?? Which CV bullet follows the roadmap's formula best?
+ Wrote 15 detections, each tested using Atomic Red Team
- Passionate team player with a strong interest in cybersecurity
- Responsible for various security tasks during my university degree
- Good knowledge of SIEM, EDR, and some cloud platforms
=> Action + tool + result + number. It's specific, checkable and hard to fake.

## GitHub, LinkedIn, portfolio

| Surface | What good looks like |
|---|---|
| **GitHub** | A profile README saying what you do and linking six pinned projects. Every repo: README with a diagram, setup, findings and limitations, licence. No secrets, no dumps of other people's tools, no abandoned tutorials |
| **LinkedIn** | A headline naming your target role, e.g. "Cybersecurity Master's student · SOC & detection engineering · building a detection-as-code portfolio". About in three short paragraphs. Featured: three projects. One post a month: a write-up or lab result, never engagement bait |
| **Portfolio site** | GitHub Pages plus a static generator. Sections: Projects, Write-ups, Talks, CV, Contact. It exists so a recruiter who won't open GitHub still sees the work |
| **Write-ups** | One a month, 800–1,500 words: the problem, what you tried, what failed, the evidence, what you'd change. Several interview invitations will come from these |
