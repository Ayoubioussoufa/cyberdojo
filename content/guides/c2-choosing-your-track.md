The goal isn't to find the objectively best path, because it doesn't exist. It's to find where your interest, your demonstrated aptitude and the local market overlap, then commit long enough to get good.

## Step 1: Keep an energy log (Months 9–18)

After every study session, write one line: date, domain, **flow** (1–5: did time disappear?), **pull** (1–5: did you want to keep going?), **traction** (1–5: did you improve quickly?), and one sentence on what you liked or hated. Average the scores at the end of each domain month. Self-reported "interest" is unreliable. A log of actual energy isn't.

## Step 2: Score the finalists

| Criterion | Weight | How to score it 1–5 honestly |
|---|---|---|
| Interest / energy | 30% | Your energy log average, not what sounds impressive at a party |
| Demonstrated aptitude | 25% | How fast you finished labs unaided, project quality, feedback from a practitioner |
| Market access | 20% | Real job adverts you could apply to within 12 months. Fewer than 10 is a warning |
| Entry accessibility | 10% | Does the field hire juniors directly, or only after 2–3 years elsewhere? |
| AI resilience / leverage | 10% | How much entry-level work is being automated, and how much leverage AI gives a skilled person |
| Lifestyle fit | 5% | Shifts, on-call, travel, client pressure, remote options: the things people quit over |

Multiply each score by its weight and compare. If two paths land within 10%, choose the one with more junior openings in your market. The second job is much easier to choose than the first.

## Step 3: Reality-check with humans and adverts

- **Read 20 job adverts per finalist.** Extract repeated requirements into a list and mark what you already have. That list is your Months 19–24 syllabus.
- **Do 2–3 informational interviews per finalist.** Ask: what does a bad day look like, what do juniors underestimate, what would you learn now if you were starting? The script is in [networking and internships](/guides/h2-networking-and-internships/).
- **Do one 48-hour trial:** a mini project in that path. If you resent it on day two, that's data.

## Step 4: Commit like an adult, review like a scientist

- **Primary track: 70% of your time. Adjacent secondary: 30%.** Adjacency matters: detection pairs with SOC, cloud with DevSecOps, AI security with AppSec.
- **Write a one-page commitment memo (Month 18):** the track, the reasons, target roles, the six-month plan, and what evidence would change your mind.
- **Review at Months 21 and 24.** Change only on evidence, such as sustained dislike or no market access, never on one bad week or a hype cycle.
- **It's a two-way door.** Most professionals change specialization at least once. Fundamentals travel with you. Only the top 20% of the skill stack is track-specific.

The seven tracks are: **A** SOC/IR, **B** Red team, **C** Cloud, **D** AppSec/DevSecOps, **E** Security and detection engineering, **F** AI security, **G** GRC.

## Signals, as hypotheses, not horoscopes

| You love… | Consider |
|---|---|
| Writing code and arguing about design | AppSec, DevSecOps, security engineering |
| Investigating and narrating what happened | IR, DFIR, detection engineering, CTI |
| Finding the one thing that wasn't supposed to be possible | Pentest / red team |
| Making systems tidy, automated and measurable | Cloud security, security engineering |
| Organising people, processes and evidence | GRC, audit, AI governance |
| Being early on new technology, and living with ambiguity | AI security, on top of one of the above |

## Common mistakes

- **Choosing red team because it looks glamorous.** It's the most competitive entry market and the most exposed to automation for routine work.
- **Choosing by salary tables from another country.** Pay only matters where you can actually be hired.
- **Choosing nothing.** Twelve more months of "a bit of everything" produces a CV that fits no role. A wrong choice at Month 18 is easily corrected. No choice isn't.
- **Confusing the specialization with the first job.** You may enter through a bridge role like IT support, NOC, junior developer or GRC analyst, and reach your track from inside.
