## Networking, the version that works for introverts

- **Give first.** Publishing write-ups, answering questions in forums and sharing resources creates more contacts than any amount of "connecting".
- **Where:** OWASP chapters, BSides and local conferences, university clubs and CTF teams, professional Discord and Slack groups, LinkedIn comments on work you've read. In North Africa, GITEX Africa in Marrakech is the largest annual gathering of the sector.
- **Volunteer:** help run a CTF, moderate a community, review a friend's report, take notes at a meetup. Organisers know everyone.
- **Keep a contact log:** name, where they work, what they care about, last contact, next follow-up. Two follow-ups a week is enough.

### Informational interview script

> I'm a cybersecurity Master's student building a portfolio in [track]. I read your work on [specific thing]. Could I ask you three questions for 20 minutes?
> (1) What does a bad day in your role look like?
> (2) What do juniors underestimate?
> (3) If you were starting today, what would you learn first?

Send a short thank-you afterwards saying what you changed because of the conversation. That's what makes people remember you.

## Internships and first roles

- **Timing:** apply 3–5 months before the start date. For a summer internship after your first Master's year, that means Months 7–9. For a final-year internship (PFE), Months 13–16.
- **Where to look:** company career pages (banks, telecoms, insurers, retailers), consultancies and the Big Four, MSSP/MDR providers, integrators, software companies, national CERTs, public agencies and remote-friendly startups. University career services and alumni are underused and effective.
- **Cold outreach:** three short paragraphs. Who you are and what you study. One specific thing you built, with a link. A precise ask: "is there an internship or shadowing opportunity in your SOC between February and July?" Attach nothing, link everything.
- **Research route:** ask a professor about an ongoing project. AI security, detection evaluation and cloud security have low barriers for original student work. A measured experiment with a public dataset can become a student paper or a well-read post.
- **Bridge roles are legitimate.** IT support, NOC, system administration, junior developer or junior DevOps positions lead into security within 12–18 months, often faster than waiting for a perfect junior opening.
- **Apply at 60–70% match.** Junior adverts describe a wish list, not a requirement list. Never disqualify yourself: let them do it.

@term MSSP = Managed security service provider: a company that runs security monitoring for other companies.
@term MDR = Managed detection and response: an outsourced service that detects and responds to threats.
@term PFE = Projet de fin d'études: the final-year internship project in francophone engineering and Master's programmes.

## Proving ability without professional experience

| Signal | How you build it | Why it convinces |
|---|---|---|
| Reports and write-ups | 16 projects, one public write-up a month | It's the actual work product of the job |
| A lab you can rebuild | Scripted AD, SOC and cloud environments | Shows operational ability, not just tool use |
| Measured results | Detection coverage, attack-success reduction, posture deltas | Numbers are checkable and rare in juniors |
| Open-source contributions | A Sigma rule, a tool fix, documentation | Public proof that someone else accepted your work |
| Responsible disclosure | VDPs or a CVE in a small project, strictly in scope | Real-world testing and professional conduct |
| Teaching | A workshop, a meetup talk, a tutorial series | The strongest evidence of understanding |
| Volunteer work | A review for an association, with written permission | Real stakeholders and constraints |

## Turning university projects into portfolio pieces

- Pick coursework with a security angle and reframe it: problem → threat model → implementation → testing → results → limitations.
- Add what academia usually omits: a README, a diagram, tests, CI, and a security review of your own work.
- Write the blog version explaining one interesting decision. That post, not the grade, is what recruiters read.
- Check your university's intellectual-property rules, get teammates' consent, and strip any data or credentials.
