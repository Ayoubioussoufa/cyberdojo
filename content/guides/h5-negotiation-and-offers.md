@term CERT (computer emergency response team) = A team that handles security incidents, often for a whole country or sector.
## The Moroccan and francophone market

If that's where you're looking:

- **Largest employers of junior talent:** consultancies and the Big Four, banks and insurers, telecoms, integrators and MSSPs, offshoring service centres, and the public sector.
- **Demand:** GRC, ISO/IEC 27001 and audit skills are in unusually high demand relative to offensive roles. A bilingual French/English candidate with a real risk register and a technical portfolio is a strong profile.
- **Regulation worth naming in interviews:** the national cybersecurity law 05-20 and the DNSSI baseline overseen by the DGSSI and its national CERT, personal-data law 09-08 with the CNDP, plus GDPR and NIS2 when the employer serves European clients.
- **Remote work** for European employers is realistic for cloud, AppSec and detection roles. English writing and a public portfolio matter more than geography there.
- **Keep both CV versions,** French and English, and rehearse technical vocabulary in both. Interviews often switch mid-conversation.

@term GDPR = The EU's General Data Protection Regulation, its main personal-data law.
@term NIS2 = An EU directive setting cybersecurity obligations for important organisations.

## Negotiation basics

- Know market ranges for your role and city before the first call, not after.
- Think in total compensation: base pay, bonus, benefits, training budget, certifications paid.
- When asked for your expectation, give a researched range and say what it's based on.
- Ask what's negotiable. It's normal, and the worst likely answer is no.

## Questions to ask before accepting

Build a "questions for them" list: team structure, tooling, on-call, mentoring, how they use AI, what a junior's first 90 days look like, and how people get promoted.

## If you're still searching at Month 24

Widen geographically (remote, francophone Europe), widen role titles, and consider a three-month internship or apprenticeship as a deliberate entry route.
