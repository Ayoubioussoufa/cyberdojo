Anyone can summarise a vendor's blog post. What employers want is intelligence that changes something: analysis, honest confidence, and detections you've proved work.

## Step 1: State your question

Pick a campaign covered by several independent public reports. Write your **intelligence requirement**, as in [lesson 66](/courses/0066-threat-intelligence/): "Would our logistics company detect this group, and where are we blind?" Choose a real one, and be specific about who needs the answer.

@done I've written one clear requirement, and listed at least three independent sources.

## Step 2: Store your indicators

@help misp guided

**MISP** is a platform for storing and sharing threat intelligence. Run it with Docker:

```bash
git clone https://github.com/MISP/misp-docker.git
cd misp-docker
cp template.env .env
docker compose pull
docker compose up -d
```

Log in at `http://localhost` with `admin@admin.test` and `admin`, then change that password straight away. It only runs on your own machine, so keep it that way. Create an event for your campaign, and add its indicators, with a TLP tag on each.

@done My MISP event holds the campaign's indicators, with TLP tags.

## Step 4: Write the assessment

Write 5 to 8 pages, using the language from [lesson 66](/courses/0066-threat-intelligence/). Separate what's known from what's judged. Name your confidence and why, and list what you couldn't find out. Avoid attribution unless your sources strongly support it, and mark the report with TLP.

@done My report states confidence levels and collection gaps.

## Step 5: Turn it into detections

@help sigma recall

**Your task:** derive five detections from the behaviours, write them as Sigma rules, and test them in your lab. Note which techniques you **can't** detect with your current logs, as that's a useful finding in itself.
