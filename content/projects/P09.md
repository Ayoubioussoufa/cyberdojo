No tool in this project will save you. What matters is thinking clearly about one specific company. Vague GRC work is worthless, so the first step is to make your company real.

## Step 3: Build the risk register

@goal Write your scales in business terms, then build a register of at least 15 risks: full risk statement, asset, inherent score, control, residual score, owner, treatment and review date. Use a formula for the scores.
@search iso 27005 risk statement example | risk register template likelihood impact

Write down your scales first, in business terms, as in [lesson 60](/courses/0060-risk-assessment/). Then add at least 15 risks to a spreadsheet, with these columns:

| ID | Risk statement | Asset | Inherent L × I | Control | Residual L × I | Owner | Treatment | Review date |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Write each one as a full risk statement: who or what, through which weakness, causing what harm. Use a formula for the scores. Be ready to defend each rating: if a director asks why it's a 4, you need an answer.

@done My register has 15 or more risks, with owners and treatments.

## Step 4: Find the gaps

@goal Go through CIS Controls IG1 or a CSF 2.0 profile line by line, and record where you stand. Turn the gaps into a 90-day plan, ranked by risk reduction, with rough costs and owners.
@search cis controls implementation group 1 | nist csf 2.0 organizational profile

Pick [CIS Controls IG1](https://www.cisecurity.org/controls) or a [CSF 2.0](https://www.nist.gov/cyberframework) profile, as in [lesson 61](/courses/0061-frameworks/). Go through it line by line: is this done, partly done, or missing? Give evidence for each.

Turn the gaps into a **90-day plan**, ranked by risk reduction, with rough costs and owners.

@done I've got a gap assessment and a dated 90-day plan.

## Step 5: Write three policies

@goal Write three short policies that people could really follow, such as acceptable use, access control and incident reporting. Give one to a friend, and ask what it requires of them.
@search acceptable use policy example small company | writing security policies plain language

Write three short policies that people could really follow, such as acceptable use, access control and incident reporting. Use the structure from [lesson 62](/courses/0062-policy-privacy-and-the-midpoint/). Each needs a purpose, the rules, an owner, a review date and how to get an exception. Keep each to one page or so.

Test them: give one to a friend. Can they tell you what they must do?

@done My three policies are short, specific, and someone else understood them.

## Step 6: Write for the director

@goal Write one page for a non-technical director: the top risks in plain words, what you recommend, what it costs, and what happens if nothing's done. Publish the whole pack, with this page first.
@search board level cyber risk summary example | cisa tabletop exercise packages

Write one page that a non-technical director could act on: the top risks in plain words, what you recommend, what it costs, and what happens if nothing's done. This page goes first in your pack, and everything else is an annex. Use the three-sentence method from [lesson 60](/courses/0060-risk-assessment/).

If you can, run a [CISA tabletop exercise](https://www.cisa.gov/resources-tools/services/cisa-tabletop-exercise-packages) with friends, and write up what you learned.

@done My pack opens with a one-page summary, and it's published as a clean PDF or repository.
