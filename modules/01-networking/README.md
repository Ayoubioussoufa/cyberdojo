## Modules

### Foundation

- [Netcat and socat](01-netcat-and-socat.md): A socket you can type into. Netcat and socat strip networking down to one idea, which makes them the best way to learn protocols, and a classic sign of an intruder.
- [The IP layer and ICMP](02-ip-layer-and-icmp.md): Every field in an IPv4 header exists for a reason, and most have been abused. Learn TTL, fragmentation and ICMP as a defender who can read them.

### Intermediate

- [TCP internals and attacks](03-tcp-internals.md): How TCP turns an unreliable network into a reliable stream, and what happens when someone attacks the state, the numbers or the trust behind it.
- [Layer 2 attacks and switch defences](04-layer-2-attacks.md): Local networks were designed for trusted neighbours. See how VLANs, the MAC table, spanning tree and DHCP are abused, and which switch features answer each attack.
- [DNS in depth](05-dns-in-depth.md): The internet's phone book trusts answers that it can barely verify. Poisoning, rebinding, tunnelling and takeover, then DNSSEC and encrypted DNS, and what each really fixes.
- [Packet analysis in depth](06-packet-analysis-in-depth.md): From a kernel-level capture filter to fingerprinting TLS clients without decrypting them. tcpdump, BPF, tshark, TLS key logs, JA3 and JA4, and flow records.
- [Scanning in depth](07-scanning-in-depth.md): What each Nmap scan type asks a target, why the answers differ, how the Internet gets scanned in minutes, and what all of it looks like from the defender's chair.
- [LAN and legacy protocols](08-lan-and-legacy-protocols.md): SMB, RDP, SNMP, NFS, FTP, Telnet, NTP and name-resolution fallbacks. The services that fill real internal networks, how they get abused, and how to harden them.
- [SSH internals and forwarding](09-ssh-internals-and-forwarding.md): How SSH builds a secure channel, why trust starts with a question you probably click through, and how its forwarding turns one login into a private network.

### Advanced

- [Proxies, pivoting and tunnels](10-proxies-pivoting-and-tunnels.md): How traffic gets relayed through hosts and hidden inside other protocols, why one foothold can open a whole network, and how to catch east-west movement.
- [IPv6 security](11-ipv6-security.md): IPv6 replaces ARP, DHCP assumptions and address scanning with new mechanisms and new attacks. It's probably already running on your network, whether you meant it to be or not.
- [Routing and BGP security](12-routing-and-bgp.md): The Internet's routing rests on networks believing each other's announcements. How BGP works, what hijacks have done, and how RPKI is slowly replacing belief with proof.
- [Denial of service](13-denial-of-service.md): Availability attacks come in three forms, and each fights a different resource. Reflection, amplification, application floods and HTTP/2 Rapid Reset, and the defences for each.
- [QUIC and HTTP/3](14-quic-and-http3.md): A new transport over UDP with encryption built in. Why it was created, what it hides from network defenders, and how to keep visibility without breaking the modern web.
