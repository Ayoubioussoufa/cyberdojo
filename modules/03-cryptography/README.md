## Modules

### Foundation

- [Cryptography foundations](01-crypto-foundations.md): What it means for a cipher to be secure. Kerckhoffs's principle, attacker models, classical ciphers, the one-time pad, and the formal definitions behind modern cryptography.
- [The maths behind public keys](02-crypto-maths.md): Modular arithmetic, groups, Euler's theorem and the discrete logarithm. Just enough number theory to read RSA and Diffie-Hellman properly, with everything small enough to run.

### Intermediate

- [Block ciphers and modes](03-block-ciphers-and-modes.md): Inside AES, why a block cipher alone is useless, how ECB, CBC, CTR and GCM differ, and why authenticated encryption is the only default that makes sense.
- [Hash function internals](04-hash-function-internals.md): How a hash is built, why SHA-256 has a length-extension problem that SHA-3 doesn't, what the birthday bound means, and how collisions became real.
- [RSA in depth](05-rsa-in-depth.md): How RSA really works, why the textbook version is broken, what padding does, and how a single error message kept breaking TLS servers for twenty years.
- [Diffie-Hellman and elliptic curves](06-diffie-hellman-and-elliptic-curves.md): How two strangers agree on a secret in public, how elliptic curves shrink the same idea, and why a single reused number can leak a signing key.
- [PKI in depth](07-pki-in-depth.md): Why revocation barely works, how public logs caught rogue certificates, why lifetimes are collapsing to 47 days, and how to run mutual TLS and a private CA.
- [Password storage under attack](10-password-cracking.md): What a stolen password database is worth, seen from the defender's chair. Why speed decides everything, how hardness settings are chosen, and how to audit your own hashes.
- [Key management](11-key-management.md): Cryptography turns every problem into a key problem. The key lifecycle, KMS and HSM, envelope encryption, rotation, secret sharing and everyday tools such as GnuPG and age.

### Advanced

- [TLS internals](08-tls-internals.md): Every message in a TLS 1.3 handshake, how its key schedule works, what twenty years of attacks taught the designers, and how to audit a real server.
- [Common crypto flaws](09-breaking-bad-crypto.md): Strong algorithms, broken systems. Padding oracles, malleable ciphertexts, weak randomness and timing leaks, seen as design mistakes to find in review and fix for good.
- [The crypto frontier](12-modern-crypto-frontier.md): Post-quantum migration, end-to-end messaging, zero-knowledge proofs, multi-party computation and homomorphic encryption. What each one solves, and what's actually deployed.
