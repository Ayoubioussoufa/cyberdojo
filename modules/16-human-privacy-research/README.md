# 16 Human factors, privacy and research foundations

People, privacy, and how a Master's student reads and does security research.

Generated by `pnpm audit:coverage`.

## Basics, on the 24-month roadmap

- [0054-phishing-analysis](../../content/lessons/0054-phishing-analysis.md)

## Modules

### Foundation

- [Social engineering](01-social-engineering.md): Why people comply with attackers. The psychology behind phishing, voice and SMS scams, business email compromise and helpdesk fraud, and the process controls that actually stop them.
- [Reading and doing security research](07-reading-and-doing-research.md): How a Master's student gets from blog posts to primary sources. Top venues, reading a paper in three passes, judging claims, reproducing work, and picking a thesis topic.

### Intermediate

- [Usable security and culture](02-usable-security.md): When security fails because of how people work, the design is usually at fault. Warnings, passwords, phishing tests, and how to build a culture where reporting is safe.
- [Privacy engineering](03-privacy-engineering.md): Security guards data, and privacy asks whether you should hold it at all. Minimisation, why anonymisation fails, k-anonymity, differential privacy and tracking.

### Advanced

- [Anonymity and Tor](04-anonymity-and-tor.md): Encryption hides what you say, not who you're talking to. How onion routing works, what it doesn't protect, why it matters for rights, and how defenders handle it at work.
- [Formal methods for security](05-formal-methods.md): Proving properties instead of testing for bugs. Protocol verification with ProVerif and Tamarin, design checking with TLA+, verified systems such as seL4, and the limits of a proof.
- [Distributed systems and blockchain security](06-distributed-systems-and-blockchain.md): How machines agree when some of them lie. Byzantine faults, consensus, Sybil resistance, and what smart contract failures teach about code that can't be patched.
